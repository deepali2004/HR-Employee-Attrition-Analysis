# HR Attrition: Business Insights

## Objective

Analyze employee attrition patterns and identify workforce segments that may warrant further investigation.

## Key Observations

Based on the supplied dataset and dashboard:

1. **Age group:** Employees aged 26–35 have the highest attrition count.
2. **Salary slab:** The salary slab up to 5k has the highest attrition count.
3. **Job role:** Laboratory Technicians have the highest attrition count among job roles.
4. **Overall attrition:** 237 of 1,470 employee records are marked as attrition, corresponding to approximately 16.12%.

These observations describe the number of attrition cases, not the probability of leaving within each group.

## Business Recommendations

### Compare Attrition Rates

Calculate attrition rates within each age group, salary slab, department, and job role. This will help distinguish groups with high counts from groups with high attrition rates.

### Investigate Employee Satisfaction

Explore whether attrition patterns differ by job satisfaction, work-life balance, and other available employee-experience measures.

### Review Tenure Patterns

Analyze attrition by years at the company to identify tenure ranges that may benefit from additional support or retention initiatives.

### Validate Before Acting

Use the findings as a starting point for further investigation rather than assuming that age, salary, or job role directly causes attrition.

## Limitations

- The observations are descriptive and do not establish causation.
- Group sizes must be considered when comparing attrition counts.
- The gender chart does not currently reconcile with the supplied CSV.
- Recommendations should be validated with additional analysis before implementation.

## Future Analysis

- Calculate group-specific attrition rates.
- Compare departments against the overall attrition rate.
- Explore relationships between attrition, satisfaction, overtime, and tenure.
- Present validated findings with supporting calculations.
